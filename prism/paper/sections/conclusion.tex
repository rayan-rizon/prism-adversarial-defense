\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \prism, a multi-layer runtime defense that combines
persistent homology, split conformal prediction, BOCPD+CUSUM campaign
detection, and a routed mixture-of-experts recovery module to detect and
remediate adversarial inputs with distribution-free FPR guarantees.
On CIFAR-10 (5 seeds, $n{=}1000$), \prism Full achieves mean detection TPR
$0.918$ at matched FPR $0.074$ across FGSM, PGD-40, and Square, $0.938$ on
canonical CW-L2, and $1.000$ on the AutoAttack ensemble's final
adversarials (per-sub-attack breakdown, weakest standalone Square
$0.706$ @ L1, in App.~\ref{app:autoattack}) --- $+33.6$~pp mean TPR over the
strongest baseline detector (Mahalanobis). On campaign streams \sacd flags
sustained $\rho{=}1.0$ attacks within $2.6 \pm 0.5$ queries with zero
false-alarm activation on clean-only streams up to $1{,}000$ queries
(the deployed $C^*{=}5.0$ admits its first spurious trigger only beyond
$\approx 1{,}800$ steps; $C^*{\geq}8$ or periodic reset removes this,
App.~\ref{app:l0_stress}). A slow-ramp or minimal-perturbation dual-adaptive
attacker defeats the per-image-reset monitor (miss $0.27$--$0.47$), but
session-level monitoring---where the change detector persists across the
attacker's query stream---restores the full-system miss to ${\le}0.03$
(\S\ref{sec:campaign_robust}), so C3 is scoped to session-granularity
monitoring. On L3-rejected PGD
adversarials, a \tamsh router trained directly on the L3 pool (logistic
regression on final-layer activations) recovers $28.7\%$ of inputs ---
$9.1\times$ the uniform $1/K$ control ($3.2\%$) and $2.1\times$ the
lightweight $H_0{+}H_1$ Wasserstein gate ($13.7\%$), matching the
prior-augmented upper bound ($29.3\%$) without its hardcoded prior and
clearing the pre-registered C4 gate
(Appendix~\ref{app:tamsh_diag}); the large margin over the uniform
control isolates the routing-signal contribution \emph{on PGD}. This
uniform-is-degenerate result is PGD-specific: on CW and Square the
uniform $1/K$ mixture matches or beats the topology gate
(Appendix~\ref{app:recovery_multi}, e.g.\ Square $+47.2$ vs $+43.5$~pp,
CW $+47.0$ vs $+39.0$~pp), so uniform mixing of the deployed experts ---
not the topology gate --- is the deployable cross-attack recovery
strategy beyond PGD. A prior-augmented upper-bound variant that always
routes to the PGD specialist reaches $+26$~pp and is reported as an
analysis ceiling against the per-input oracle ($0.324$ recovery,
$+29.1$~pp over passthrough), not as a deployable policy. All numbers
are generated from the locked CIFAR-10 Vast.ai and local pipelines,
with attack detection, adaptive attacks, matched-FPR baselines, campaign
streams, recovery, and ablation tables all derived from result JSONs
rather than manually transcribed values. A separate standard-attack
CIFAR-10/ViT-B/16 transfer run reaches $1.000/1.000/0.9998$ TPR on
FGSM/PGD-50/Square at FPR $0.0828$; this run supports transformer
transfer for standard attacks but does not claim ViT latency, CW-L2,
AutoAttack, or adaptive robustness.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The primary limitation is computational: persistent homology over 
high-dimensional activation tensors requires subsampling 
(150 points per layer in the current configuration), which may weaken detection for attacks
that specifically preserve local topology while altering global structure.
Single-sample end-to-end detection latency on an RTX 4090
(Table~\ref{tab:latency}, $n=200$) is $39.78$\,ms mean / $41.26$\,ms
p95 on CIFAR-10 and $50.35$\,ms mean / $55.90$\,ms p95 on CIFAR-100,
both well below the $100$\,ms real-time target; CPU-only deployment is
significantly slower because persistent-homology computation on
high-dimensional activation tensors does not vectorise.
Point-cloud subsampling in \texttt{TopologicalProfiler} uses
deterministic MD5-based hashing (rather than a stateful RNG) to ensure
consistent scoring across calibration and inference passes.
The conformal calibration assumes the clean test distribution matches
the calibration distribution; we \emph{measure} the cost of violating
this on CIFAR-10-C (\S\ref{sec:cert_shift}): realised FPR inflates to
$3.9\times$--$28\times$ the certified $\alpha$ depending on tier
(severity-dependent, worst on noise corruptions), so the certificate
must be read as in-distribution FPR control. Online shift-aware
recalibration on a small benign slice of the shifted stream restores
realised FPR to ${\approx}\alpha$ (mean L1 $0.39{\to}0.09$;
\S\ref{sec:cert_shift}, Table~\ref{tab:cert_restore})
\cite{tibshirani2019conformal}.
The adaptive-attack evaluation in \S\ref{sec:adaptive} now uses an
ensemble-complete BPDA surrogate that targets activation matching, DCT
energy, softmax entropy, logit profile, StabilityV2, input gradient norm,
and the side-quadratic logistic score. This is still a surrogate attack:
the deployed detector includes non-smooth pieces (topological diagrams,
hard tier thresholds, and hash-deterministic subsampling), so the attack
optimizes differentiable proxies rather than the exact discrete decision
rule. Under the strongest confirmed setting ($\lambda{=}10$, $5$ seeds,
$n{=}200$/seed), all-input TPR falls to $0.479$, but the backbone attack
success rate also falls to $0.552$; among successful adversarials,
\prism detects $0.866$, leaving a $0.074$ undetected-success rate over
all attempted inputs. The small-sample per-seed FPR estimates are noisy
(one seed has L3 FPR $2/200=0.010$), while the pooled confirmation is
L3 FPR $0.005$ and the independent calibration gate is L3 FPR $0.003$.
We therefore treat this as a strong adaptive stress test, not a proof of
adaptive robustness.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Highest-priority directions, in order:
(1) rerunning the ensemble-complete adaptive attack on a fixed disjoint
$n{=}1000$ clean/adversarial EVAL subset and extending it to CIFAR-100,
WRN-28-10, and ViT backbones;
(2) strengthening the adaptive optimizer beyond the current BPDA
surrogate with larger budgets, alternative side-channel losses, and
explicit expectation-over-transformation repeats where randomness is
introduced;
(3) extending the current ViT encoder-block activation monitor with
transformer-specific attention-head and MLP-block activation patterns;
(4) certified robustness integration by combining \prism with randomized
smoothing \cite{cohen2019certified} for joint detection-plus-certified
prediction guarantees;
(5) online recalibration of the conformal thresholds to handle clean-side
distribution shift;
(6) closing the residual \tamsh recovery gap: a learned router trained
directly on the L3-rejected pool (logistic regression on final-layer
activations) already matches the prior-augmented upper bound and is
$9.1\times$ the uniform control (Appendix~\ref{app:tamsh_diag}); richer
multi-layer or expert-capacity improvements are needed to close the final
gap to the
oracle ceiling.

\paragraph{Cross-architecture and cross-dataset generalisation.}
Section~\ref{sec:multi_backbone} reports the same protocol on
CIFAR-10/WRN-28-10 (mean pool TPR $0.975$, FPR $0.073$) and
CIFAR-100/ResNet-18 (mean pool TPR $0.830$, FPR $0.077$), both run
on the same 5 seeds and same calibration scheme. It also reports a
standard-attack CIFAR-10/ViT-B/16 transfer run (FGSM/PGD-50/Square only)
with $1.000/1.000/0.9998$ TPR at FPR $0.0828$, and a $224\times224$
ImageNet-100/ResNet-50 resolution-scaling run (FGSM/PGD-50/Square, 3
seeds) with $0.924/1.000/0.852$ TPR at FPR $0.065$; the conformal
certificate stays valid there despite a $\sim$100$\times$ larger clean
score scale, since the guarantee is a calibration-score quantile.
The full per-component CIFAR-100 breakdown (adaptive PGD,
channel-mask ablation with FPR disclosure, campaign, recovery, latency)
is in Appendix~\ref{app:cifar100}. The TAMSH C4 recovery contribution
does not transfer to the 100-class setting (within $1.4$~pp of
passthrough) and is reported as appendix-only on CIFAR-100.

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
\prism is a defensive tool designed to improve the reliability 
of ML systems under adversarial conditions.
We do not foresee misuse potential beyond standard ML security research.
